One app, four homes

Made with each platform's own toolkit

Verso looks and moves as the apps around it do, because it is made with what they are made with. Underneath, every platform shares one core in Rust: the Immich client, the drive's layout and the arithmetic that places the grid's dates, so Verso behaves alike everywhere.

A closer look

Your whole library, and a viewer that feels at home

Your whole library

The timeline by year, by month or every photo, with a date scroller along its edge; albums, people, places and the map, memories, tags, folders, shared links, partners, duplicates, the archive, the trash and the Locked Folder.

A viewer that feels at home

Photos follow a swipe, the arrow keys or the touchpad; videos and Live Photos play; the photo's people, place, camera and file are a scroll down.

Search

By what photos show, by text in them, by file name or description, narrowed by people, places, dates and cameras.

Organise and share

Favorites, albums, stacks, dates and locations, the archive, the trash and the Locked Folder; albums shared with people and by link; photos and folders imported from the device.

Sign in your way

With your server's single sign-on, with email and password, or with an API key. The sign-in stays in the system's keyring or Keychain.

Your account and server

Your profile, password, devices and API keys, and for an admin, the server's administration.

With other Immich apps on the Mac and Linux, Verso takes part in Immich Crosslink: Show in opens the same photo, album, person or place in another app that takes part, and those apps can show theirs in Verso.

Verso Drive

Your library as folders, for every other app

Switch on Verso Drive, and your library is a drive in Files on Linux, the iPhone and the iPad, and in Finder on the Mac. Attach a photo to an email, open a video in your editor, or drop new pictures into Upload to send them to Immich, without downloading the library first.

  • Thumbnails from the server: browsing a folder never downloads the photos in it.
  • Originals on demand: a file is fetched only when something opens it.
  • Off until you want it: Verso Drive is a switch in Verso's settings.

On the iPhone and the iPad

Back up your photos, then free up space

Verso backs your photos up to Immich in the background, with their Live Photos, edits and favorites, and hidden photos into the Locked Folder. Once they are safely in Immich and old enough, Free Up Space offers to remove them from the phone: never without asking, and keeping your favorites, the albums you choose and anything recent.

  • Checked before anything goes: only photos Immich holds whole leave the phone, and they wait in Recently Deleted for 30 days.
  • Verso in Messages: pick a photo from your library straight into a message.

What Verso needs on Linux

New enough
GNOME 49's libraries or newer (libadwaita 1.8 and GTK 4.18): Fedora 43 and newer, Ubuntu 25.10 and 26.04 LTS, Debian 14, Arch and other rolling distributions.
Too old
Ubuntu 24.04 LTS and what is built on it (Linux Mint 22, Pop!_OS 24.04, Zorin OS 18), Debian 13, Fedora 42 and anything older. There Verso says so when it starts, and the Flatpak, which brings GNOME's own libraries, runs instead, without Verso Drive.
Verso Drive
FUSE 3. Verso's menu in Files needs nautilus-python, and the top bar icon a tray: on GNOME, the AppIndicator extension.

Privacy

What Verso sends, and to whom

Verso talks to the Immich server you sign in to. There is no account with us, no analytics, no trackers and no advertising, and Verso looks for no updates of its own.

Your sign-in stays on the device

A session token or an API key, never your password: in the keyring on Linux (or a file only you can read), and in the Keychain on the Mac, the iPhone and the iPad, on that device only.

Encrypted unless you choose otherwise

Verso tries https first, and never falls back to plain http for a server beyond your own network, so nobody on the way can take the encryption off. A certificate your system does not trust is never accepted by itself.

Maps

On Linux the map comes from where your Immich server's map setting points, as in Immich's web app, and those hosts learn the areas you look at; nothing is fetched until a map shows. On the Mac, the iPhone and the iPad the map is Apple's, and looking up place names with Apple Maps is off until you switch it on.

Your library changes only when you ask

A favorite, an album, a date or a place change when you change them. Backing up never deletes on the server, and Free Up Space removes photos from the phone only after you review them and iOS asks.
